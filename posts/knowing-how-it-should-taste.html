Knowing how it should taste

Building taste by eating. Sharing it with recipes.

Saved October 29th 2026. Topics: food cognition

At Fuchsia Dunlop’s talk at Vivienne Culinary Books, she described the most important part of her work: going to China and eating a lot. She said people laugh at her as if she is joking when she says this, but she is absolutely serious. She has to get a feel for a cuisine and a recipe, and build her taste for both.

The goal is knowing how it should taste. Eating, not measuring, is my most important research. Written recipes are a backup; the senses are the primary source.

Fuchsia Dunlop (Vivienne Culinary Books), paraphrased from my notes.

But what about a reader who can’t spend months in China? Who doesn’t know what Mapo Tofu should taste like? How can she write a recipe for them?

She described the culinary institutes of Sichuan as obsessed with creating canonical recipes for a dish.

That’s not her goal. She wants a recipe accurate and forgiving enough for a reasonable cook to make the dish.

Precision helps someone who doesn’t yet know the destination. It helps a cook understand what a dish should taste and feel like.

Fuchsia Dunlop (Vivienne Culinary Books), paraphrased from my notes.
Source:
Dunlop, Fuchsia, in conversation with Heather Arndt Anderson. “Author Talk: Five Tastes with Fuchsia Dunlop.” Vivienne Culinary Books, Portland, Oregon, October 28, 2026.
